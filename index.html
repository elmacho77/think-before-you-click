Article 01

Think Before You Click: Recognizing Phishing

Phishing is when someone pretends to be a trusted person or organization to get you to reveal information or click a harmful link. Scammers often use emails or texts that look like they came from a bank, delivery service, or online store.

Watch for messages that create panic or pressure you to act immediately. A message saying your account will be closed unless you click a link is a good reason to slow down, not rush.

  • Don't open unexpected attachments or links.
  • Contact the company through its official website or an app you already use.
  • Never share a one-time verification code with someone who contacts you.

Takeaway: If something feels urgent or unusual, verify it another way before responding.

Article 02

Protecting Your Personal Information

It's easy to share more information than we realize. Social media posts, online forms, and shopping accounts can reveal details about our lives that scammers may use to target us.

Before posting or filling out a form, ask whether the information is really needed. A legitimate website should have a clear reason to request sensitive details.

  • Review who can see your social media posts.
  • Limit personal details such as your address, birthday, and travel plans.
  • Check app permissions and remove access you don't need.

Takeaway: Share less when you can, and regularly review your privacy settings.

Article 03

Passwords and Multifactor Authentication

Passwords protect our online accounts, but reused or easy-to-guess passwords can put several accounts at risk at once. A password manager can help you create and store a different strong password for each account.

Multifactor authentication (MFA) adds another step to signing in, such as an authenticator app or security key. Even if someone learns your password, that extra step can help keep them out.

  • Use long, unique passwords for important accounts.
  • Turn on MFA, especially for email, banking, and social media.
  • Keep your phone, browser, and apps updated.

Takeaway: Start with your email account, since it is often used to reset other passwords.

Article 04

Online Shopping Scams

Fake online stores and misleading ads can make a deal look too good to pass up. Scammers may copy real store logos, post fake reviews, or advertise popular products at unusually low prices.

Before buying, check the store's reputation using independent sources. Look carefully at the website address and return policy, and be cautious if a seller insists on gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency.

  • Search for the seller's name along with words like “scam” or “complaint.”
  • Compare prices with established retailers.
  • Use payment methods that offer purchase protections when possible.

Takeaway: A few minutes of research can save a lot of trouble.

Article 05

What to Do If You've Been Scammed

Even careful people can fall for convincing scams. If you think you've shared information or sent money to a scammer, taking action quickly may help limit the damage.

  • Contact your bank or payment provider immediately if money was involved.
  • Change affected passwords and enable MFA.
  • Check your accounts for unusual activity and save relevant messages or receipts.
  • Report scams to the FTC. If your identity was stolen, visit IdentityTheft.gov for a recovery plan.

Takeaway: Act quickly, document what happened, and use trusted reporting resources.
